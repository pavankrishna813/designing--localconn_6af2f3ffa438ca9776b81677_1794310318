# LocalConnect — Usability study plan and reporting template

Prepared 10 November 2026 • **No usability sessions or participant results exist yet.**

## Study objective

Evaluate whether residents can find a suitable event, understand participation details, register, recover from a form error and retrieve the reservation. Use a selected interactive prototype with fictional event data. An expert review is useful preparation but must not be reported as participant testing.

## Method

Run 5 moderated, approximately 20-minute sessions with adult residents across the hypothesised user segments and different levels of technical confidence. Seek relevant access needs without assuming them from age. Let participants choose a comfortable device and assistive technology where supported. Explain that the interface is being tested, not the participant. Participation is voluntary; obtain permission before recording; use IDs P01–P05 and sample form details.

Do not teach the interface. Ask participants to think aloud and use neutral prompts such as “What are you looking for?” Record unsolicited versus moderator-assisted completion separately.

## Tasks and success criteria

| Task | Neutral scenario | Observable success |
| --- | --- | --- |
| T1 Discover | You are free on Saturday morning and want a free activity in this neighbourhood. Find a suitable event. | Participant independently reaches an event matching the scenario |
| T2 Evaluate | Decide whether this event would work for you. Explain the details you would check. | Correctly identifies date/time, venue, price and relevant access/eligibility information |
| T3 Register | Reserve one place using the sample details provided. | Reaches explicit success once and can explain reservation status |
| T4 Recover | Use an incomplete email, try to continue, then correct it. | Notices the error and recovers without losing other input |
| T5 Retrieve | You return later and want to find your reservation. | Independently finds the reservation in My events |

After each task ask perceived ease from 1 (very difficult) to 7 (very easy). After the session ask what felt unclear and what would prevent actual attendance. Do not interpret preference alone as proof of usability.

## Analysis and decision rules

Calculate independent completion as independently completed tasks divided by attempted tasks; always show counts and the denominator. Calculate median task time only across comparable completed tasks and explain exclusions. Keep assisted outcomes separate. A small convenience sample supports directional design decisions, not population estimates.

Prioritise observed issues by impact and recurrence: critical = core task blocked with no recovery; major = substantial confusion or assistance; minor = recoverable delay. Accessibility blockers can merit priority even if observed once. Maintain an issue log with task, evidence, severity, proposed change, owner and retest outcome.

Proposed design targets, not results: at least 4 of 5 participants independently complete the core discovery/registration/retrieval journey; all tested critical errors have clear recovery; no unresolved observed critical blocker. Targets may be revised with the evaluator before testing.

## Report template to complete after sessions

1. Study date, prototype version, participant recruitment and limitations.
2. Task outcomes with counts, timing and ease ratings.
3. Observed issues with participant IDs and supporting evidence.
4. Changes selected and the rationale for priority.
5. Before/after frames and change log.
6. Retest method and outcomes; unresolved risks.

## Pre-test design checks — planned, not findings

Check whether selected date and heading agree, all filters affect results, map content has a list equivalent, text scales, focus order is logical, forms preserve input, and confirmation matches My events. Generated concept images may contain text inconsistencies; correct these during implementation and verify the rendered screens.
